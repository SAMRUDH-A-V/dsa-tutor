GREEDY ALGORITHMS

Greedy Algorithm:
A greedy algorithm makes the best-looking choice at each step with the goal of reaching a global optimum.

Common problems:
1. Activity Selection
2. Jump Game
3. Assign Cookies
4. Gas Station
5. Minimum Number of Arrows to Burst Balloons

Activity Selection:
Choose the maximum number of non-overlapping activities.

Optimal approach:
Sort activities by their finishing time.
Always choose the activity that finishes earliest and does not overlap with the previously selected activity.

Time Complexity: O(n log n)
Space Complexity: O(1) excluding sorting space.

Jump Game:
Determine whether the last index can be reached.

Greedy approach:
Maintain the farthest index that can currently be reached.
If the current index is beyond that point, the end cannot be reached.

Time Complexity: O(n)
Space Complexity: O(1)

Common mistakes:
1. Assuming every optimization problem can be solved greedily.
2. Making a locally good choice without proving it leads to an optimal solution.
3. Forgetting to sort when the greedy strategy requires ordering.
4. Confusing greedy with dynamic programming.

Hint progression:
Hint 1: Can you make a choice that is best at the current step?
Hint 2: What information should you maintain after making that choice?
Hint 3: Can you prove that the local choice never prevents an optimal solution?
